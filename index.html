What is Pip?

Not a summary. Not a lesson. One story, all the way through.

Most news is written for adults. It assumes you already know the backstory. Pip does not.

Each edition is called The Six. It picks one topic and asks what, why, when, how, where and who. It starts with something you can picture. It ends with what to watch for next.

Pip is a crow. The kind who is always on the roof, always watching, always the first to know. Pip does not teach. Pip found something interesting and wants to show you.

Why "The Six"? Rudyard Kipling once wrote about six honest serving men who taught him everything he knew. Their names were What and Why and When and How and Where and Who. Every edition of Pip asks all six.

From Just So Stories, first published in 1902.
Who is it for?

Readers aged 10 to 17. And the grown-ups at their dinner table.

Written for a smart 10-year-oldThe topic is made simple. The reader is not. Real words, explained when needed.
Deep enough for a 17-year-oldThe early pages are easy. The later pages go further. You earn the depth by reading on.
India in every storyOur readers are Indian. Each edition shows where India fits in the story.
Something to think aboutEach edition carries open questions with no right answer. They work well at dinner.
No opinions on politicsPip reports what happened and what it means. Readers make up their own minds.
FreeNo app. No login. No ads. Each edition is a single page that opens in a browser.
